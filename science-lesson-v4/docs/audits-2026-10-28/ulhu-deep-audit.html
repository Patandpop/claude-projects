Quick wins

Small fixes, mostly under an hour each. Most are bugs that quietly affect fairness or trust.

Make the shop locks count

BugSmall

Locked Eldritch and side-work items are meant to be long-term goals; right now 9 of them are open from day one.

The shop list writes locks two different ways. The server only understands one of them, so half the locks (three "needs level 15", one "level 10" and five "outside-work ticks") are ignored. The existing test passes because it happens to pick one of the items written the other way.

game-shop.json has {type:"level",min:15} and {type:"outside_work",count:N} on 9 items; gLock_ in Game.gs reads lock.level / lock.outside_work only. Normalise in gShop_(); add a test that every locked item is refused at level 1.

Catch up the day before any tick, and refresh when the app comes back

BugSmall

Streaks are the heart of the game, and a wrong reset is the fastest way to stop caring about one.

The day only rolls over when the whole app loads. If the phone page was left open overnight, a morning tick happens first and the catch-up runs after it, so yesterday's miss can drop the multiplier a step or reset the streak even though today is ticked. The same open page also shows yesterday's lit ticks as if they were today's, and tapping one then "unticks" nothing on the server.

Call gRollover_(gWorld_()) at the top of gameTick/gameUntick (it returns early when lastDay is current). In game-ui.js, on visibilitychange or focus, reload with gameLoad when the stored D.today is not today.

Make Undo after fainting exact

BugSmall

Your rule is that every curse Undo reverses exactly; this is the one case where it doesn't, and it's the most painful one.

If a curse takes Ulhu to 0 health, he loses a level and the last 7 days of runes. Undo gives back the health only. The screen briefly shows the old level, then the server's reply drops it again. Undoing a curse also leaves the card one shade redder than before.

gameUntick doesn't reverse gDie_ (store lost runes, old level and old XP in the tick note and restore them); for habits it also skips restoring prev.v (value).

Stop names turning into spreadsheet formulas

BugSmall

One ritual named "+20 press-ups" or "-Netflix" would show as an error in the sheet and could break that row.

Google Sheets reads any cell starting with + - = or @ as a formula. Text like "1/2" or "10" can also be turned into a date or number. This applies to ritual names, quest steps, Hoard rewards, Vespers details, your own chips and review text.

Guard in gAppend_/gSet_ for string values: prefix "'" when /^[=+\-@]/ or when the value would parse as a date/number in a text column.

Keep the sigils for reduced motion

BugPhoneSmall

If the phone's "reduce motion" setting is ever on, every curse is logged with no sigil, and High Vespers' "where the curses came from" stays empty.

The blood trail is skipped for reduced motion, and the sigil choice is skipped with it. The four sigils should still rise, just without the flying blood.

game-ui.js around line 987: BLOOD.open is bypassed when prefers-reduced-motion matches. Open it in a still mode instead (game-blood.js already reads the setting).

Don't let curses and blessings count as life goals

BugSmall

The life bonus is meant to reward real life rituals; a Netflix slip shouldn't move it.

The life bonus (+5 for the first life ritual of the day, +10 more at the third) counts every tap on anything filed under Life. Netflix is a Life omen, so a slip quietly skips the +5 and makes the +10 arrive early.

gPay_ life bonus: filter todayTicks to goals with kind daily/todo/termly (skip kind habit).

A size alarm and a slimmer paste

SafetySmall

Finding the 380 KB limit by a paste that won't save, mid-build, is the worst time to find it.

The bundler should warn at about 340 KB per file and strip the long explanatory comments from the pasted copies (the repo keeps them). That alone should cut 15 to 25% from every file. See the size chart below.

planner/bundle.js: no size check today. Add a byte check per output and a comment-strip pass (local, free; terser with compress off, or a careful line-comment strip).

Check the Apps Script time zone is London

Risk1 minute

If it isn't, weeks in summer time shift by a day and the "lessons built" and "behaviour logged" auto-ticks can quietly miss.

The game counts days in London time, but the planner reads its week dates in the project's own time zone. In Apps Script, Project Settings, the time zone should say London.

loadAll_ in Code.gs uses Session.getScriptTimeZone(); gSnapshot_ mood uses gNow_().getHours() (script zone) rather than TZ.

Make each tap save faster, one at a time

BugMedium

The screen reacts at once, but the save behind it takes seconds; tap five blessings quickly and the last ones can fail with "Not saved".

Every tap re-reads around 20 sheet tabs and loads the whole planner (weeks, lessons, equipment, results) up to three times, about 2 to 4 seconds. Fast taps queue on the server and give up after 10 seconds. Fix both ends: the server reads each thing once per tap and only the planner parts it needs; the page sends taps one after another instead of all at once.

Memoise gWorld_ per execution and load only Data + Weeks; memoise gRows_ per execution (clear on write); parse tick notes once. withLock_ waitLock(10000) in Behaviour.gs. game-ui.js send(): chain calls on one promise.

No-signal ticks: keep the right day, never twice

BugMedium

A tick saved on the phone at 11 pm and sent in the morning counts for the wrong day, so yesterday shows as missed.

The server stamps the day when the tick arrives, not when you tapped. And a slow reply that looks like "no signal" is saved and sent again later, so a curse could cost health twice. Each tap should carry its own time and a one-off label the server checks.

game-ui.js queue()/flush(): add client time + action id; gameTick accepts a tap day up to ~36 h old; reuse the existing ref "never pays twice" check for the id.

Never miss twice for every-few-days rituals

gRollover_ filters out g.everyN; a miss = everyN days pass after the last tick with no new tick.

Habit ideas from the research

All optional, all things you'd switch on yourself. Nothing here reminds, nags, decides for you or works after 7 pm.

A tiny version of each ritual

IdeaSmall

On a bad day the choice becomes "the tiny version" instead of "nothing", which is how streaks survive.

BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits and Atomic Habits' two-minute rule both say the smallest version still builds the habit. Each ritual could carry an optional minimum ("Read one page") that keeps the streak and pays as Tiny. You pick it from a long-press; it's never offered on its own.

An "after I..." line on a ritual

IdeaSmall

Habits tied to something you already do stick far better than habits tied to willpower.

Fogg's anchors and Clear's habit stacking: an optional line under the ritual, such as "after I make the first coffee". Shown quietly, never sent as a reminder.

Turn the one change into an if-then plan

IdeaSmall

If-then plans roughly double follow-through in studies, and you already collect the "if" part.

Gollwitzer's implementation intentions (a medium-to-large effect across 94 studies, Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006). High Vespers already knows your top curse sigil and each curse's antidote, so "Your three lines and one change" could offer the shape "When [top Scene], I will [antidote]" for you to edit or ignore.

Think again about how hard fainting hits

Your call

Research on slips says a big punishment after a lapse makes the next lapse more likely, the "might as well" spiral.

Fainting costs a level and a week of runes, about 1,000 at a steady pace, which is roughly an Eldritch item. Habitica players often name "death" as the moment they quit. You chose this rule on purpose, and Absolution already softens it. Options if it ever feels too harsh: a fixed loss, or letting an Absolution still win part back after a faint.

Marks past 66 nights

IdeaSmall

After 66 nights the stone stops changing, but habits keep strengthening long after.

Lally's 2010 study gives 66 days as the middle, with a range of 18 to 254, and found one missed day made little difference (which backs never-miss-twice). A look-only change at 100, 200 and 365 nights, such as the cracks glowing gold, with no extra pay.

Fresh starts without resets

IdeaSmall

People restart habits most readily at natural new beginnings.

The fresh-start effect (Dai, Milkman and Riis, 2014): new half term, the first day back, the Monday after High Vespers. A quiet "new chapter" line in the Oracle on those days. Nothing resets.

Careful with paying more for what you enjoy

Your call

Paying for things you already love can make them start to feel like work.

The overjustification effect (Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999). Reading and time with Martha already pay double. Suggestion: don't raise life pay further; let life rituals earn mainly through identity votes and the Hoard, which fits self-determination theory's point that choice and identity keep motivation your own.

A sealed-evening mark

Needs your OK

Your big goal is not working evenings; a quiet sign that you managed it helps more than any rule.

If nothing from Teaching was ticked after 7 pm, Vespers shows a lit candle. No pay, nothing blocked, nothing said if you did work. This was proposed before and you were unsure, so it only happens if you want it.

Small, rare surprises

IdeaSmall

An occasional unexpected moment keeps a game fresh far longer than bigger numbers do.

After a week with every ritual kept, Ulhu sometimes finds a trinket in the deep (a free cosmetic) or says a rare line. Cosmetic only, rare, never a nag. The shareable weekly card for Martha, already on your wish list, is the other half of this.

Blessings have no daily ceiling

Your call

Every blessing tap pays, so one often-tapped blessing can quietly become the biggest earner.

Not a problem while you're honest with yourself, which is the point of the game. If prices ever start to feel cheap, a soft limit (say the fifth tap of a blessing in a day pays nothing) is the lever.
