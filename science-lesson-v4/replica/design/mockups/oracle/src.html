The Oracle

Round 2: your pick B2, the rose window, rebuilt as real leaded stained glass. Three ways to build the glass (R1 to R3) and three middle marks (M1 to M3), on B2 and then on B3. The marks are cut into the glass: lead runs from each point of the star, moon or middle mark out to the edge of its piece, so the glass around it is shaped by it. Round 1 is folded at the bottom. Five weeks of example data: 28 Sep to 1 Nov 2026, with a half-moon break from Thu 22 to Sat 24 Oct and High Vespers every Sunday.

Round 3: B2 in real stained glass

The rose window you picked, now cut with the same glass engine as the blessing tiles, with carved stone tracery framing each day. A gold star means all or most rituals kept, the middle mark means some, and a dim moon means none. Monday is the spoke at the top, so you can read every Monday straight down from the top to the middle. The glass is lit from behind and glows into the lead. Tap any piece for the full night.

B3 in real glass: the five lancets

The same glass work on B3: one tall lancet per week, Monday at the top, Sunday's High Vespers in the round pane at the foot. Read across the top row for every Monday. Each pane is cut like a blessing tile round its mark.

Earlier options (round 1)

Idea A: hanging panes

Each night's Vespers is kept as the day-in-glass pane (red curses on the left, blue blessings on the right, gold stars where rituals were kept). Brighter, gold-edged panes are nights where all or most rituals were kept. Sunday's High Vespers pane is larger and crowned.

Idea B: the cathedral window

The old Oracle's stars become glass jewels with the same meanings. Lead lines join days kept in a row, like the old star lines.
