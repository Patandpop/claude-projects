# Big goals: research (29 Oct 2026)

Five ranked apps per goal with features to steal. For the grilling before each goal is built (see FABLE-WORKPLAN.md "Big goals"). Goal 5 (colleagues) was not researched. Rules kept: no paid AI, suggest never decide, no microphone, pupil data stays in his Google account.

## 1. A memory map for every class
The north star: pupils remember the spec. Every vote, Form and homework shows what each class remembers; the Do Now suggests itself from the weak spots and you tick what you want.

### 1. Anki (FSRS scheduling)
Free flashcards whose maths models how fast each thing is forgotten.
- A fading score per spec point per class: green when fresh, fading towards red as time passes. That grid IS the memory map.
- You set one number (say 80%). Spec points that drop below it are flagged as due for retrieval.
- A right answer pushes the next review further away; a miss brings it back sooner. Free maths, no AI.
- A termly check: what the map predicted vs what the class actually got.

### 2. Carousel Learning
UK retrieval-quiz platform, popular in secondaries.
- Each question gets a "how useful to ask now" score. The Do Now builder shows the top 10 and you tick 6.
- After a Form closes: the 3 most-missed questions ready to go through next lesson.
- Whiteboard mode already matches your MCQ slides; the votes just need logging against spec points.
- Pupils self-mark the Do Now; you enter only "how many got it".

### 3. Eedi (Diagnostic Questions)
MCQs where every wrong answer is a named misconception.
- Each wrong MCQ option carries a misconception tag, so a vote for C logs "thinks current gets used up", not just "wrong".
- Opening next week's lesson shows "this class most often picked..." before you teach it.
- Questions tagged to spec sub-points (4.2.1.3), not only topics.
- A Do Now on the older topics a new one depends on.

### 4. Educake
UK science quiz platform for homework and revision.
- Red/amber/green grid: classes or pupils down the side, spec topics across; tap a cell to see the questions behind it.
- Each pupil's own 3 weakest spots, for support or chasing.
- Completion and lateness feed the "pupils to chase" list on the day screen.

### 5. Plickers
Class voting with printed cards scanned by the teacher's phone.
- Each class page opens with the class average and the 3 most-missed questions.
- The same MCQ compared before and after teaching.
- No pupil devices, so it fits your corner-swipe voter idea.

**Top 5 steals:** The fading-score grid (Anki); Top-10 suggested Do Now you tick (Carousel); Misconception tags on wrong options (Eedi); Red/amber/green grid you tap into (Educake); 3 most-missed per class (Plickers).

**Watch out:** Copy the ideas only: pupil data in those companies' clouds would leave your Google account. Never auto-fill a Do Now.

## 2. One app that runs your day
No work in the evenings. Each morning one screen shows what matters instead of hunting through tabs.

### 1. Sunsama
Calm daily planner built on a morning ritual and an evening shutdown.
- The morning starts with yesterday's leftovers: unbuilt lessons and unmarked work, one tap each to do, move or drop.
- Everything is pulled into one list of candidates; you pick what goes into today. Nothing is added for you.
- A workload check: estimated minutes against your free periods, "over by 35 min".
- A shutdown at a set time: what you finished, leftovers rolled to tomorrow. Fits the 7pm rule and could be an Ulhu ritual.

### 2. Akiflow
One inbox fed by every other tool.
- One Inbox fed by the lesson log, order sheet, Form results and the brief.
- Drag "Build 10S2 L4" onto a free period on the timetable.
- Ctrl+K quick-add: "chase 9AS homework fri".

### 3. Morgen
Calendar, tasks and notes in one view.
- "Suggest my day" drafts ghost blocks; nothing is saved until you accept each one. Exactly your "suggest, never decide" rule, built with plain rules, no AI.
- Week A/B, school events and rituals layered on one grid.
- Each task type has default minutes (build a lesson about 40, mark a set about 60).

### 4. Things 3
Polished task app famous for its Today view.
- Today split into "School" and "This evening", so rituals never mix with marking.
- Timetabled lessons show inline above the tasks for each period.
- Calm: 5 to 7 items on the morning screen, the rest folded away.

### 5. Tiimo
Visual planner for ADHD, Apple's iPhone App of the Year 2025.
- The day as a coloured strip (P1 to P6, then evening), each block coloured by class.
- A visual "next up in 10 min: 9AS, slides ready?"
- Rituals as small step chips (mark 5 books, then 5 more).

**Top 5 steals:** One inbox from every source (Akiflow); Yesterday first, every morning (Sunsama); Ghost-block suggested day you approve (Morgen); Shutdown ritual at a set time (Sunsama); Minutes vs free periods check (Sunsama).

**Watch out:** Rejected: Motion, because it reshuffles your day by itself. Skip the AI assistants in Akiflow and Tiimo (paid AI).

## 3. Ulhu as a life companion
Knows your workload as well as your habits, tells a year-long story, shows who you are becoming, and is what you open instead of scrolling.

### 1. Finch
Self-care pet bird that goes on adventures when you look after yourself.
- Once the day's rituals are done, a spawn of Ulhu sinks into the deep and comes back next morning with a relic or a line of lore.
- A home-screen widget of Ulhu sleeping or stirring: works better than notifications because it never piles up.
- A one-tap "how heavy was today" stone before 7pm.
- Shop currency buys looks, never power (already true of the Night Market).
- A bad day costs nothing; Ulhu just rests in the dark.

### 2. Duolingo
The streak app; daily users still growing in 2026.
- An Elder Sign ward, earned at depth milestones, guards one missed day (fits "never miss twice").
- Nudges name something real ("your 24-day Read streak"), once, at the hour you usually tick, never after 7pm.
- A once-a-term "the tide returns" night that restores a broken streak.
- An end-of-term Oracle window that fills in like a year-in-review.

### 3. Animal Crossing (and Stardew, Spiritfarer)
Cosy games run on the real clock and calendar.
- Music for each part of the day (morning, school, evening), silence after 7pm.
- The year-long story in chapters: each half-term is one act of the Ulhu saga.
- One odd thing washes up each day, never explained.
- A rare keeper visits the Night Market on random days.
- A mastered habit is "released to the deep" in a small ceremony, freeing its slot.

### 4. Focus Friend
Hank Green's focus timer: a bean knits while you stay off your phone. No. 1 on the US App Store, Aug 2025.
- Ulhu carves a tablet while you focus; the carving crumbles if you leave to scroll.
- Carved stones furnish his lair.
- Its whole stance is "control your attention": your open-this-instead-of-scrolling goal.
- A sad sprite says more than a sentence, which suits your no-over-explaining rule.

### 5. Daylio
Mood and activity diary in two taps.
- Year in Pixels: the Oracle becomes a year window, one pane per day.
- A "Who I am becoming" page: which rituals and omens come before your better days (simple counting, no AI).
- Tag days like marking, parents' evening or INSET, so the term page shows how workload and habits pull on each other.
- Logging stays under 10 seconds.

**Top 5 steals:** Daily adventure that returns with lore (Finch); Who-I-am-becoming page from simple counting (Daylio); Half-term story acts and time-of-day music (Animal Crossing); Earned Elder Sign streak ward (Duolingo); Focus carving that crumbles if you scroll (Focus Friend).

**Avoid:**
- Group punishment and harsh death (Habitica): critics call it the app most likely to punish you on a bad week.
- Selling streak safety, and sad-pet push messages (Duolingo). A sad sprite inside the app is fine; a sad notification is pressure.
- Long tutorials and motivational text (Fabulous). Teach through what happens on screen.

## 4. Ulhu for your friend
A version for someone who doesn't teach, maybe with a shared layer. Research finding: for two friends, a shared goal with soft stakes beats a contest; one person's slip must never hurt the other.

### 1. Habitica
The habit RPG with party boss quests.
- A shared boss: both players' rituals damage one health bar, tallied overnight.
- Soften Habitica's main complaint: a missed day slows the attack but never costs your friend health.
- A holiday or sick pause (Habitica's "Inn") that freezes both your own and shared penalties.
- Quests start by themselves; never wait for both to accept.

### 2. Duolingo
Friends Quests and Friend Streaks.
- A weekly joint goal (say 30 rituals between you) that pays both of you runes.
- A one-tap nudge that appears on your friend's Call screen ("the stars are watching").
- A shared streak only on rituals you both choose to share, with a freeze.
- Gift a one-day blessing bought with your own runes.

### 3. Finch
The gentlest social layer.
- One tap sends a tentacle wave or blessing; no chat, no typing.
- Visit your friend's lair, Ulhu and level without seeing their habits.
- Buy a cosmetic in the market and give it to them.

### 4. Strava
Social exercise log with kudos.
- One-tap kudos on each ritual or quest your friend completes (a study linked more kudos with more running).
- A small feed: "Friend beat the Swamp Wraith", never habit names.
- Keep counts private: chasing kudos stresses some users.

### 5. Apple Fitness sharing
Ring-closing and 7-day duels.
- An opt-in one-week duel scored as % of each person's own goals, so a light habit list can still win.
- A "hide me this week" switch.

**Top 5 steals:** Shared boss that never hurts your friend (Habitica, softened); Weekly joint goal (Duolingo); Tap-to-send blessings and lair visits (Finch); Kudos feed without habit names (Strava); Pause button for holidays and illness (Habitica).

**Making it work for a non-teacher:**
- Starter packs at first launch (Fitness, Study, Home, Health, Creative, Blank) instead of your teaching rituals.
- Her own habit categories, keeping the colours and icons.
- Make-a-boss: a name, a pixel sprite, a target ("30 rituals tagged Admin"), plus presets like an Inbox Hydra or a Laundry Kraken. Marking golems stay yours.
- No term dates, Week A/B or INSET; her own rest days and quiet hours instead.
- One pass of wording to take out pupils, marking and lessons.
- Market, dice and bosses unlock over the first week, so she isn't swamped.

**Privacy:** She should run her OWN copy under her own Google account, so her habits live in her Drive, not yours. For playing together, one small shared "party" sheet holds only names, levels, boss damage, kudos and the shared streak. Never habit names or health. Free.
