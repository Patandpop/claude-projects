# Six hook mechanisms. Tag every Engage with engageType (1–6). Never repeat the same type in consecutive lessons for the same class.

---

## The six mechanisms

| # | Name | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Discrepant event | Topic has a genuinely counter-intuitive result. **Take a prediction commitment first** — the gap between expectation and outcome is the hook. |
| 2 | Anchoring phenomenon | Lesson introduces a new model/concept. The phenomenon is the question the lesson answers by the end. Don't use for recap lessons. |
| 3 | Forced-choice question | Default when no strong event or phenomenon exists. Force a visible commitment (hands up / whiteboard). **Don't resolve on the Engage slide — reveal belongs at Review.** |
| 4 | Image/data without caption | A genuinely striking graph, photo, or dataset exists. Prompt: "What do you notice? What do you wonder?" Don't force a boring image. |
| 5 | Mystery / withheld info | Lesson's Learn section reveals one specific named mechanism/structure. Present the gap without naming what's missing. |
| 6 | Prior-knowledge connection | Fastest/weakest. Good for warm-ups or pairing with 1 or 3. Not a long-term default for substantial new content. |

---

## Example hooks

**1 — Discrepant event**
- *Physics:* Two balls (different masses) dropped together — both hit simultaneously. Hands-up prediction first. → forces/gravity
- *Chemistry:* Steel wool burned in oxygen — mass appears to increase. Predict first. → conservation of mass, oxidation
- *Biology:* Tardigrade rehydrating from "dead" state within minutes. → adaptation, extremophiles

**2 — Anchoring phenomenon**
- *Physics:* Sea stays cool; sand burns your feet — same sun, why? → specific heat capacity
- *Chemistry:* Flint MI water crisis — why did safe pipes suddenly leach lead? → reactivity, corrosion
- *Biology:* Tiger population collapse 40,000→1,800 (1900–1973): how many deer per tiger, plants per deer? → food chains

**3 — Forced-choice question**
- *Physics:* Car crash — solid wall vs crumpling wall, which is worse? → forces, momentum, safety
- *Chemistry:* Would you rather drink from a swimming pool, a puddle, or the sea? → water purification
- *Biology:* Does digesting steak or salad use more energy? → respiration

**4 — Image/data without caption**
- *Biology:* UK measles graph 1996–2020, axis labels hidden — predict the spike and the dip. → vaccination
- *Physics:* CO₂ Mauna Loa data, labels hidden — two patterns overlaid, what are they? → climate/greenhouse
- *Chemistry:* Damascus steel blade wavy pattern, unlabelled — what produces this? → metals, alloys

**5 — Mystery / withheld info**
- *Biology:* Man loses smell after head injury; food tastes bland but tongue is fine. What's missing? → nervous system/senses
- *Physics:* Same plug, identical-looking cables — one charges slower. What's different inside? → resistance
- *Chemistry:* Two white powders: one dissolves clear, one goes cloudy. Same test, different result. Why? → solubility

**6 — Prior-knowledge connection**
- *Physics:* Phone gets warm when charging — what's being wasted and where does it go? → energy efficiency
- *Biology:* You sweat when hot — mechanically how does sweat actually cool you? → thermoregulation/homeostasis
- *Chemistry:* Shaken fizzy drink sprays; flat one doesn't — why? → gas solubility, pressure

## Selection priority

1 or 2 (strongest evidence base) > 4 (if a striking image exists) > 3 (reliable default, any topic) > 5 (only for single-reveal lessons) > 6 (pairing/fallback only)

For mechanism 4: prefer real sourced images (Wikimedia Commons or clearly-licensed) over `imagePlaceholder` when a strong graph/photo genuinely exists for the topic.

## Question bank: the standard to aim for (Patryk approved, 2026-10-19)
Each is a real puzzle the lesson answers. None has an obvious answer, none is a trick.
| Lesson | Engage question | The lesson answers it with |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure (Y8) | "A camel and a horse weigh about the same. On soft sand the horse sinks and the camel doesn't. How?" | Wide feet spread the force over a bigger area: less pressure. |
| Distance–time graphs (Y8) | "This is a graph of someone's walk to school. For two minutes the line goes flat. What happened?" | Flat line: distance not changing, so they stopped. |
| Conservation of energy (Y10) | "A dropped ball never bounces back to the height you dropped it from. Where did the missing energy go?" | Thermal store of the ball and floor, and sound. |
| Latent heat (Y9) | "Steam at 100 °C burns far worse than water at 100 °C. They're the same temperature. Why?" | Steam gives out extra energy when it condenses on your skin. |
| Microscopes (Y9) | "Robert Hooke saw tiny boxes in cork in 1665 and named them cells. Why couldn't he see anything inside them?" | Low resolution, and cork cells are dead and empty. |
| Efficiency (Y10) | "An old bulb gets too hot to touch. An LED just as bright stays cool. Which one wastes more energy, and where does it go?" | The old bulb: most of its energy heats the surroundings. |
| Non-renewables (Y10) | "Coal is made from plants that grew 300 million years ago. Is a coal power station running on sunlight?" | Yes, stored as chemical energy by the plants. |
| Cells (Y7) | "Every living thing is made of cells. A virus has no cells at all, yet it can make you ill. So is a virus alive?" | What cells do (the life processes). |
| Energy resources | "You charge your phone from the plug. Where was that energy before it reached the plug?" | Traced back store by store. |
| Fool's gold (Y9) | "Gold rush miners called this 'fool's gold'. How could you prove it isn't gold?" | Weigh it (gold is about 4 times heavier) or heat it (sulfur smell): it is the compound FeS_2. The reveal answers HOW, not just what. |

## Hooks to avoid
- "Have you ever wondered…?" with no real puzzle in it, and "Imagine a world without…" openers.
- Fun facts the lesson never comes back to (research: "seductive details" lower recall).
- A big demo with no prediction and no explanation later: pupils remember the bang, not the science.
- Silly questions with an obvious answer, or a statement where a question should be.
- Leaving Engage out. Every standard and practical lesson has an Engage attempt, always a question (Patryk 2026-10-30). If no hook above fits, ask one from the history of the idea, from real data, or from what Patryk has just said or shown.
