# Transcribing handwriting

The transcript is the foundation of everything else. If it drifts from what Patryk wrote, the analysis criticises words he never chose and praises lines he never had. So the first job is fidelity, not tidiness.

## How to read the page

- Look at the whole page before reading a line. Find the title (if any), the margins, crossings-out, arrows, insertions above the line, and anything written sideways. Handwritten drafts often have a second thought squeezed in; the squeezed-in version is usually the one he meant to keep.
- Read each line twice: once for the words, once to check letters that look alike in his hand (a/o, e/i, n/u, r/v, cl/d, rn/m). Use the sense of the line to settle a doubtful letter, but never use the sense to swap a word for a "better" one.
- Crossed-out words are left out of the transcript unless nothing replaces them and the line makes no sense without them; then keep the crossed word inside `[struck: word]` so he can decide.
- Insertions (carets, words above the line) go where the caret points.
- Several photos of one piece: put them in the order the page numbers or the sense dictates, and say which photo each part came from if the order was not obvious.

## What to keep exactly

- His line breaks. In a poem a line break is a decision; in prose it usually is not, but keep it anyway and let the analysis step decide the form.
- His stanza and paragraph breaks (a blank line in the transcript).
- His spelling, punctuation and capitalisation, including anything that looks like a mistake. A missing full stop may be a rhythm choice. Flag probable slips later in the analysis, under a short "Possible slips" note, never by changing the transcript.
- His indents where they look deliberate (two or more spaces, or a clear step in from the margin).

## Marking doubt

- A word you can mostly read but are not sure of: `[?harbour]`, your best guess inside.
- A word you cannot read at all: `[?]`.
- A whole line you cannot read: `[? illegible line]`.
- Never leave a gap unmarked and never fill it with a plausible word. He will correct the marks; he cannot correct a silent guess.

## Presenting the transcript

- A fenced code block, so it copies cleanly into Obsidian on his phone with the line breaks intact.
- Title on the first line if the page has one. If it has none, do not invent one; the report will use the first line in quotation marks as its title.
- After the block, count the doubt marks in one short line ("3 words marked [?]") and mention any crossing-out you resolved. Then, if he only asked for the transcript, ask the single question: "Anything to correct before I analyse?" If he asked for the analysis in the same message, carry on with it and say that corrections will get the report rebuilt.

## Typed text instead of a photo

If he pastes typed text there is nothing to transcribe. Do not echo it back. Go straight to the analysis, and use his pasted text, unchanged, as the transcript in the report.
