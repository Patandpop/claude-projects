On Coming Back

I stopped writing the year I started teaching. Nobody told me to. It was simply that the hours which used to hold a notebook now held a pile of exercise books, and the part of me that noticed things, the salt on a handrail, the way a bus driver says goodnight, started noticing marking instead. Noticing is a muscle and I let it go.

What I did not expect was that it would not come back on its own. I assumed, in the way one assumes about a childhood language, that it was in there somewhere, waiting. It was not waiting. It had packed up and left and taken the furniture, and when I sat down last month with a new notebook and an old pen I produced, in forty minutes, a single sentence about the weather that even the weather would have found dull.

So this essay is an attempt to understand what the coming back actually requires. Not inspiration, which I have come to distrust, and not discipline either, which sounds like a thing you do to a dog. Something in between, something more like a standing arrangement with yourself: I will be here, at this table, at this time, whether or not anything arrives. The arriving is not my department. The being here is.
